Gate story (Part 1 essay, in one paragraph)

Order of events: the m gates open first (fast Na⁺ activation) → Na⁺ rushes in and v shoots toward ENa = +45 mV. The h gates then close (Na⁺ inactivation) ending the upstroke, while the slow n gates open and K⁺ flows out, repolarizing the membrane and briefly overshooting to a hyperpolarized after-potential (v approaches EK). A second shock at 10 ms fails because h is still low (Na⁺ channels inactivated) and n is still high (K⁺ conductance elevated) — the absolute refractory period. Later, a stronger-than-threshold stimulus can fire again (relative refractory period). In hyperkalemia the depolarized rest potential fires once then blocks (inactivated Na⁺ channels) — why IV KCl is lethal; in hypokalemia the hyperpolarized rest slows the approach to threshold. With a sustained current, spike frequency codes stimulus intensity until adaptation silences the train.
